An installation you walk into

Echoes of Light was an immersive installation showcased at Dubai Design Week, designed to merge architecture, art and technology into a living spatial experience.

Hybrid Xperience asked for something that could not be photographed in a single frame, a work that only resolves as you move through it. The brief was less about an object and more about a condition: a room that behaves.

The work invited visitors to step into a choreographed world of light, shadow and reflection, a space that responded to movement and perspective. No two paths through the installation produced the same experience.

/02 Concept
  • Light as Memory
  • Shadow as Material
  • Reflection as Space

Light, held like a memory

What if light behaved the way memory does, intangible yet powerful, constantly shifting and reshaping the way we perceive our surroundings?

That single idea set every decision that followed. Through a carefully composed environment of illuminated planes and reflective surfaces, visitors experienced how light can transform space into a narrative.

Shadow was treated as material rather than absence. Reflection was treated as architecture rather than finish. Together they let a small footprint read as an endless one.

/03 The Walkthrough
  • Four Movements
  • Visitor Path

Four movements, one path

The installation was authored as a sequence. Each step through it changed the composition, approach, threshold, immersion, echo.

01
The Approach

Seen from a distance

Before entry, the work reads as a glow, a soft, slowly shifting field visible across the hall. It draws people in without instruction: no signage, no queue, just a change in the light.

02
The Threshold

Crossing into it

At the edge, the illuminated planes resolve into individual surfaces and the cast patterns land on the body. The visitor stops being an audience and becomes part of the composition.

03
The Immersion

Inside the choreography

Within the work, light, shadow and reflection respond to movement and perspective. Reflective surfaces multiply the planes so the space reads far deeper than its footprint.

04
The Echo

What stays after

On exit, the pattern persists, an afterimage rather than an object. The work ends the way it began: as something remembered rather than something seen.

/04 The Tech Side
  • Procedural Design
  • 3D Modeling
  • Fabrication

Grown in code, built by hand

Procedural design workflows generated the plane geometries and light-catching patterns, then resolved them into buildable 3D models.

We iterated the system against simulated light until the cast shadow behaviour matched the intent, tuning density, spacing and angle rather than drawing each surface. The generative model was the design tool, not a rendering step.

The resolved geometry was translated into fabrication drawings and built structures, then installed and lit on site, a physical realisation of a system born in code.

The generative model, running
/05 From Drawing to Site
  • Nesting
  • Mesh
  • Assembly

Every layer cut flat, hung curved

The finished piece is a stack of dyed mesh panels. Each one was unrolled from the model into a flat profile, nested for cutting, then hung from a timber grid so gravity gave it back its curve.

The drawings below are the working set: the nesting sheet that packs every panel onto the roll, the top view of the batten grid that carries them, and the layer elevation that fixes the order front to back. Colour in those drawings is sequence, not finish.

On site the structure went up first as a bare white grid, read as scaffolding until the mesh arrived. The panels were hung one at a time in order, and the piece only became itself once the last layer went on and the projectors were aimed.
