TRT Metaversethe broadcaster, stepped into virtual space.

ILLUSORR designed TRT's first immersive metaverse environment, reimagining the role of a global broadcaster in virtual space. One main space that expands into a space per channel, where audiences explore programming, cultural initiatives and live broadcasts at full scale.

01 In the press

A public broadcaster announced a world.

TRT published the collaboration itself. The launch ran on TRT World and TRT Arabi, and the Turkish trade press covered it as a first for the market: a national broadcaster commissioning a purpose-built metaverse rather than renting a plot in someone else’s.

That mattered for the design. The environment had to survive being written about, screenshotted and broadcast, not just walked through.

04 The main space

One space, designed to be added to.

ILLUSORR designed the main TRT space first, and designed it as a host. Four arms reach out from a central broadcast core, each arm ending in an open edge rather than a wall, so a new space can be attached without touching what already works.

The geometry does the arguing. A rectangular plan can be extended twice before it reads as a corridor. This one grows in six directions and stays legible from above, which is how a broadcaster with more than a dozen channels can keep expanding without a redesign.

05 Twin and extension

A twin of what exists. Then the part that cannot.

Every space in the world does two jobs. It carries a function TRT already performs, so staff recognise it and can run it on day one. Then it does the thing the physical version cannot: change scale, change language, change lighting state, or hold an audience that would not fit in a building.

The broadcast floor

A working studio: desk, gallery, running order. In world the same floor drops its ceiling, wraps the feed around the full 360, and lets the audience stand inside the set instead of watching it through a frame.

The shop

A retail counter that behaves like one, with stock, prices and a queue. Then the stock becomes wearable, the shelf becomes infinite, and a character from a series can be standing next to the thing it comes from.

Digital twin
New reality
The gallery

A foyer with a hanging wall, which is what a broadcaster’s cultural arm already has. In world the wall is not a wall: the exhibition can be re-hung between two broadcasts, in as many languages as there are channels.

06 One becomes many

The main space is the unit, not the whole.

TRT runs fourteen television channels and nineteen radio stations. A single environment could never speak for all of them, and a separate build for each would fragment the brand. So the main space was designed as a cell: it clones, it keeps its circulation logic, and each copy is retuned to the channel that occupies it.

08 Inhabitants

Someone has to be in the room.

A broadcaster’s space is only as convincing as the people standing in it. ILLUSORR designed the avatar system alongside the architecture, so scale, silhouette and lighting were resolved together rather than borrowed from a marketplace.
