01 The Brief

Fashion is leaving the fabric behind.

A generation dresses its photos before it dresses itself. DressX asked for a capsule that could only exist digitally: silhouettes no pattern-cutter could produce, materials no mill could weave, physics tuned for the camera instead of the street.

The answer was Morphellum, a three-piece collection where each garment takes one impossible idea and wears it: inflated air, carved stone, quilted colour.

02 The Collection

Three garments, three impossibilities.

Helium is a gown that behaves like it is inflated. Opulence is carved like sandstone and moves like silk. Machimatch is a quilted coat whose colour field never repeats. Each is shown the way DressX sells them: in the round.

03 Inspect

Turn it in your hands.

The garment as it ships: a real mesh you can orbit, zoom and inspect from any angle.

04 The Cards

Twenty drops, one language.

Every garment shipped with its own card: name, description and render, built on one template so the collection reads as a single release.

05 The Direction

Sculpture first, garment second.

The visual language came from parametric masks: layered splines and soft transitions studied before a single garment was cut. The masks set the rules, the wardrobe obeyed them.

06 The Proof

Impossible to cut, simple to wear.

Every rib is a single continuous spline, offset and swept until the garment holds its own shape without a seam, a dart or a stitch. Nothing here could be patterned flat.

Rendered on a runway rather than a turntable, because that is the test: the collection had to read as clothing in motion, not as a sculpture that happens to be worn.
