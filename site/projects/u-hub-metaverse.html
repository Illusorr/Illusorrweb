01 The brief

One company, four hundred brands.

Unilever does not have a product, it has a portfolio. A single virtual space that tried to speak for all of it would have said nothing, and one space per brand would have been unmanageable before it launched.

So ILLUSORR designed by category instead. One arrival plaza, then a space per business group, each with its own light, material and pace, all built from the same plan so a new brand can be placed without a rebuild.

02 The plan

A plaza, and four wings off it.

Seen from above the world is a single figure: a ring at the centre with four petals reaching out of it. The ring is the arrival, where every visitor lands and every route begins. Each petal is a category, and each petal is the same shape, so the world stays readable from the air and from the ground.

Top view · central plaza with four wings
03 The film

The whole world, in one pass.

One continuous walkthrough: the mark assembling, the descent into the plaza, then each wing in turn. It was the piece that made the plan legible to people who had never worn a headset.

04 The spaces

Five categories, five rooms.

Each business group got a space shaped around how its products are actually chosen: browsed, tried, explained, cooked or grabbed. Same plan, different rules. Click a category to go in.

05 Brands in the world

The portfolio, placed not pasted.

Brands enter the world as architecture, not as logos on a wall. Each one gets a volume with its own colour and material inside its category, which keeps the parent world coherent while letting a brand be recognisably itself.

06 Inhabitants

A world for staff before it was for shoppers.

U-Hub was built to hold thousands of employees and partners at once, so the avatar system was designed alongside the architecture: scale, silhouette and lighting resolved together rather than borrowed from a marketplace.

“A portfolio is not a place. So we built the place first, then let the portfolio move in.”
